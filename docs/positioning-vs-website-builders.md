# Why Hire Us Instead of Wix? — Positioning & First-Customer Plan

_An App Idea LLC · strategy working doc · 2026-11_

> **The short answer:** Builders sell a page. We sell a business that runs
> better online, and an owner who knows how to run it. If a client only needs
> a page, a builder is fine and we should say so. Our clients need more than a
> page.

---

## 0. Assumptions (correct me if any are wrong)

1. **Market:** Denver metro (in person) for local businesses; funded founders remote. See §4.3.
2. **"Entrepreneurs with funding"** covers two groups: (a) people with a
   grant, a CDFI/SBA loan, a pitch-competition win or accelerator money, and
   (b) early-stage founders with friends-and-family or angel money. They have
   budget, but they also have **reporting requirements and a deadline**.
3. **"Technical strength varies"** means some builds go beyond what you can do
   alone today. The plan below handles this with **tiered offers and a

   match it.

## 1. The honest problem

DIY builders (Wix, Squarespace, Framer, Shopify, and now AI site generators)
have made "a decent-looking page" close to free. **If we compete on making
pages, we lose on price, every time.**

What they *don't* remove:

| What a builder gives | What it leaves to the owner |
| --- | --- |
| Templates | Deciding who the site is for and what it should get people to do |
| A drag-and-drop editor | 20–40 hours of the owner's time, the most expensive resource they have |
| Basic SEO fields | A real local-search strategy (Google Business Profile, reviews, schema, location pages) |
| App marketplace | Connecting booking, payments, CRM and email into one workflow that actually works |
| "Accessible themes" | Responsibility for accessibility (ADA/WCAG) legal risk, which stays with the owner |
| Hosting | Lock-in: most builder sites can't be exported or moved |
| A help center | A person who knows their business and picks up the phone |

## 2. What we sell that builders can't

### 2.1 Outcomes, not pages
- Conversion-first structure and **copy written in the customer's language**
- Analytics with goals set up from day one, plus a monthly **plain-English
  report** ("42 calls from the site, up 18%")
- Local SEO done properly: Google Business Profile, review flow, schema,
  speed, location and service pages

### 2.2 Custom functionality & integrations (the technical moat)
- Booking, quoting, deposits, client portals, memberships, calculators
- Wiring the site into the owner's tools (Square/Stripe, QuickBooks,
  calendars, email, CRM), so a lead becomes a booked job with no copy-paste
- Automations: speed-to-lead texts, review requests, invoice reminders
- **Mobile apps and AI** (already on our service list), which a builder can't touch

### 2.3 Performance, accessibility, ownership
- Fast on a cheap phone over a weak signal, which is how many customers browse
- WCAG 2.2 AA as standard practice, which reduces ADA demand-letter risk
- **The client owns the domain, the code and the accounts.** We put this in
  writing.

### 2.4 Capability transfer: our real differentiator
The README already says it: *"then teaching owners to run what we build."*
No builder or typical agency does this well. Package it:
- A recorded walkthrough and a one-page "how to run your site" guide
- One or two training sessions: editing content, reading analytics, replying
  to reviews
- The pitch: **"You won't be dependent on us. You'll choose to keep us."**

### 2.5 Cultural fluency & trust
For Black and brown owners, the trust gap with agencies is real: being
upsold, talked down to, or given a site that doesn't look or sound like them.
We offer:
- Brand, imagery and copy that reflect the owner and their community, without
  forcing them into a generic template look
- Plain-English explanations, no jargon, and transparent pricing
- **Pointing them to capital and visibility channels**, such as directories
  and programs that highlight Black-owned and minority-owned businesses, and
  helping them meet grant and lender requirements (see §3.2)

> ⚠️ Lead with competence, and let representation reinforce it. "We're good
> *and* we get it" converts. "Hire us because we're aligned" alone does not.

## 3. The two segments

### 3.1 Established small business owners (Black & brown-owned focus)
- **Typical state:** social media only, a link-in-bio page, or a dated
  builder site. Revenue comes from word of mouth.
- **Pain:** missed calls and DMs, no online booking or payments, invisible on
  Google Maps, too busy to deal with it.
- **What they value:** time back, more bookings, someone they trust.
- **Good-fit verticals to test:** salons and barbers, beauty and wellness,
  restaurants, food trucks and catering, home services and trades, childcare,
  fitness, cleaning services, professional services (tax prep, insurance,
  real estate).
- **Price sensitivity:** high. Use tiered packages and monthly payment plans.

### 3.2 Funded entrepreneurs
- **Typical state:** has money and a deadline (a launch, a demo day, a grant
  milestone), and often only an idea plus a Canva logo.
- **Pain:** "I have $X and N weeks, and I don't know what to build first."
  Afraid of wasting the money.
- **What they value:** scoping discipline, speed, credibility for investors
  and funders, **documentation they can show their funder**.
- **Offer hook:** a paid **Discovery / Product Sprint** (1–2 weeks) that turns
  the idea into a scoped MVP plan, clickable prototype and budget. Many
  grants and accelerators will pay for this kind of line item.
- **Price sensitivity:** lower, but they expect accountability: milestones,
  invoices and progress reports.

### 4.1 Selling each tier: the "why", in the customer's words

Use these lines on the pricing page, in proposals and on calls. Each one
answers the customer's question "why should I pay for this?"

| Tier | Customer-facing pitch |
| --- | --- |
| **Start Right** | "Already want to use Wix or Squarespace? Do it right the first time. We set it up and get you on Google Maps in one session, so you don't lose a weekend to it. Upgrade later and your fee counts toward it." |
| **Launch** | "A site built for *your* customers, not a template: online booking, payments, and showing up on Google, from $699. Pay in three parts and own everything: your domain, your logins, your code." |
| **Grow** | "You run your business; we keep your site working. Updates, security, and a plain-English report every month showing calls and bookings from your site, for less than one missed customer." |
| **Automate** | "Stop copying and pasting. We connect your booking, payments and messages so leads turn into appointments on their own. Most owners get hours back every week." |
| **Product Sprint** | "Got funding and a deadline? In 1–2 weeks you'll have a scoped plan, a clickable prototype and a real budget, ready to show your funder, before you spend big on building." |
| **MVP Build** | "We build the version that proves your idea, in milestones you can see and report on. No black box, no surprise bills." |

### 4.2 Internal pricing rules (not published)

1. **Publish starting prices** ("Launch from $699"). Being open about cost builds trust with this audience.
2. **Founding clients:** the first 2–3 clients get about 30% off, in exchange for a case study and a testimonial.
3. **Raise prices after every 3 delivered projects.**
4. **Start Right limits:** at most 2 a month; fixed scope (one session, setup, Google Business Profile); no open-ended revisions; the fee is credited toward Launch if they upgrade within 6 months; never the headline offer, used to answer the "I'll just use Wix" objection.
5. **Always sell the Product Sprint before quoting an MVP Build.**

### 4.3 Market & focus (decided)

- **Local small businesses:** Denver metro, in person: Denver, Aurora, Boulder, Golden, Littleton, Thornton, Glendale, Lakewood, Englewood, Arvada, Westminster and nearby.
- **Funded founders:** remote from day one, run as a separate, low-effort track. Show up at pitch competitions, accelerator demo days and SBDC workshops, and lead with the Sprint.
- **First 30-day niche:** beauty, barbers and wellness. Runner-up: home services and trades.

- **MVP:** about at market. There is little published local data.

> ⚠️ Gaps: no published Denver freelancer price lists, and no local prices for

## 5. Handling "technical strength varies"

Match the delivery method to the job, and be open with clients about it:

| Job complexity | Deliver with | Who builds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Marketing site | Our React/Vite stack, or Framer/Webflow when the client must self-edit | You |
| Standard integrations | Native integrations, Zapier/Make, Stripe/Square | You |
| Custom app, complex backend, AI features | Supabase/Vercel stack, with AI-assisted development | You + a **vetted subcontractor** when beyond reach |
| Specialties (brand identity, photo/video, legal) | Partner bench | Partners, coordinated by you |

Rules: you own the client relationship and QA, your contract allows
subcontractors, and you never sell a capability without a named way to
deliver it.

## 7. First-customer plan (from the `first-customers` skill, Branch B + A)

**Five-line brief**
1. **Outcome:** more booked customers and less admin time, with a site the owner understands and owns.
2. **Who:** Denver-area owner-operated businesses (priority: Black/brown-owned) and funded early-stage founders.
3. **Workaround replaced:** Instagram DMs, link-in-bio pages, missed calls, a stale DIY site.
4. **Why now:** new funding, a launch date, a slow season, a competitor showing up on Maps.
5. **Disqualifiers:** happy with their current site, no budget and no funding path, or a franchise with corporate web.

**How we find them (by hand, public sources only)**
- Business directories where owners **self-identify** as Black- or
  minority-owned, chamber listings (including Denver-area Black and Hispanic
  chambers), the Google Maps "Black-owned" attribute, and the business's own
  social pages
- Grant and pitch-competition winner announcements, accelerator cohorts and
  CDFI/SBDC workshops, all of which put you in front of funded founders
- Rely only on how owners publicly identify their own business. Never infer
  someone's race.

**Score each lead:** no site / social only = high · weak site = medium · fine = low.
Record: business, category, town, status, one visible need, published contact, source URL, date checked.

**Message template** (under 90 words, reviewed and sent by you):
> Hi [Name], I'm [you] with An App Idea, a Denver studio that builds sites
> for owner-run businesses. I noticed [specific, true gap, e.g., "booking
> only happens through Instagram DMs"]. Here's a quick fix you could do
> yourself: [one useful tip]. If you'd ever want it done for you, with
> online booking and payments in one place, I'm happy to talk for 15 minutes.
> Not interested? Just reply "no" and I won't follow up.

**7-day plan:** Day 1 shortlist ≤10 · Day 2 send top 3 · Days 3–4 reply and send a few more · Day 5 show mockups to the people who confirmed the pain · Day 6 write down their exact words · Day 7 keep going or change the pitch.

**Outreach rules:** a person sends every message · one opt-out, honored
everywhere · no scraped or bought lists · a personal line only when it's
true · CAN-SPAM-compliant email, and no cold SMS without consent.
